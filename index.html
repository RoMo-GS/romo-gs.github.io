Abstract

Robot mapping,
with motion in mind.

Sharp renderings. Compact maps.
Online reconstruction.

Online 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) is emerging as a practical representation for robotics. Real-world deployment, however, brings two challenges: rapid robot motion produces blurry observations that corrupt geometry and camera poses, while continuous mapping can grow the number of Gaussians without bound.

RoMo-GS addresses both. Instead of averaging multiple discrete sub-frames, we analytically integrate each projected Gaussian over the exposure interval, yielding O(1) complexity per Gaussian. A Hessian-based pruning score controls map growth, while joint Adam optimization refines poses, velocities, and Gaussians, followed by an online Levenberg–Marquardt step on the Gaussians.

Across public and self-collected datasets, RoMo-GS achieves competitive rendering quality with offline deblurring methods at substantially lower running time, and retains about 20% fewer Gaussians than the online MODP baseline at comparable quality.

Method

One exposure. One rasterization pass.

RoMo-GS combines exposure-integrated motion blur, joint optimization, and online density control in a tracker-agnostic mapping backend.

01

Exposure-integrated
motion blur

Analytically evaluate each projected Gaussian's exposure-time integral using the error function, under a linear screen-space motion approximation.

02

Single-sample
blur rasterization

Render a motion-blurred frame in one CUDA rasterization pass. The per-Gaussian cost stays constant instead of growing with the number of temporal samples.

03

Online optimization
& density control

Jointly refine pose, velocity, and Gaussians with Adam, apply an online LM step to the map, and remove redundant Gaussians with a Hessian-based score.
